Privacy policy

Last updated November 24, 2026

In short: Your photos and videos stay on your iPhone or go to a server that you run. The developer of Homespun never receives them. The app has no accounts, ads, or tracking, and it never sells your data. If you choose to share analytics, the developer receives anonymous usage counts and crash reports; if you send a problem report, the developer receives what you write. A separately configured optional wake service in version 1.1.0 uses a device push token only after you opt in, as described below.

Version 1.0.0 remains the public App Store release while version 1.1.0 is prepared. Features identified as 1.1.0 below are not enabled in the 1.0.0 app.

Your photo library

With your permission, the Homespun app reads the photos and videos you choose and saves compact copies to an album named "Homespun" in your library. It never changes your originals. It asks iOS to delete an original only when you choose to, and only after your server has proven it holds an identical copy. iOS asks you to confirm every deletion, and deleted items stay in Recently Deleted.

Your own server

If you connect Homespun to a server you run, such as Homespun Server or an Immich server, the app sends your originals to that server (or lets it find them in your Immich library) and downloads compact copies from it. The app connects to Homespun Server over an encrypted connection and keeps its key fingerprint with the pairing token in the Keychain, used only to talk to your server. The server's address and Bonjour service name stay on your iPhone. If its address changes, the app checks the pinned key before sending its token to the new address. A random installation ID and the last device name your server confirmed are also kept in the device-only, non-synced Keychain. They survive unpairing or reinstalling the app, but not erasing the phone. Pairing sends them to your server so an active device record can be reclaimed after your approval; the server stores only a hash of the ID. This ID is never included in analytics or problem reports and is separate from the optional analytics identifier. An Immich API key, if you use one, stays on your server. The developer of Homespun has no access to your server or its contents.

Local Network access finds Homespun Server using Bonjour (_homespun._tcp only) and connects to it. Camera access is used only to scan a pairing QR code; camera images are not stored or sent.

If you separately configure a cloud connector on your server, that server sends originals to the service you choose. OneDrive sign-in uses a code on Microsoft's website; its tokens stay on your server. The developer receives no originals or sign-in details. These connectors are configured separately on the server, not in the 1.0.0 app. Version 1.1.0 adds Premium controls for OneDrive Cloud Copies in the app; the transfer and Microsoft credentials still remain on your server.

Version 1.1.0 keeps pending backup choices, verified-copy records and recovery intent in protected local storage. Those logical records can be included in the device backup you configure with Apple. Reconstructible transfer files and resume data are excluded from that backup; pairing and wake credentials stay in device-only Keychain. Restoring a device backup does not restore a native network task or its credentials.

Find Compact Copies in version 1.1.0 sends file fingerprints (SHA-256), resource roles and sizes to your paired server to revalidate existing copies. It does not send their media or local file names. An old match alone never authorizes removing an original.

Optional background wakeups in version 1.1.0

Background Wakeups is off unless you explicitly enable it, and is unavailable in builds without a configured wake service. It is separate from sharing analytics, Premium ownership and permission to show local alerts. Your iPhone sends its Apple Push Notification service (APNs) token to the configured Homespun wake relay. Your own server receives only an opaque route and a scoped send credential, never that device token or the developer's APNs signing key.

A wake hint contains only an opaque generation and a random nonce. It contains no media, hashes, file names, server names or LAN addresses, item counts or progress. The relay sends Apple a generic background hint; the app then authenticates your server and checks its authoritative results. Apple may delay, coalesce or drop a hint. It does not carry a photo, authorize an import or deletion, or guarantee background execution.

Pending tokenless enrollments expire after one day. Active device-token enrollments expire after 90 days without a device refresh, or are removed after opt-out or an invalid-token response. Disabling wakeups fences new enrollment and retains private deletion handles until the relay acknowledges cleanup. A delayed activation cannot recreate an erased enrollment. Apple may already have accepted an in-flight generic hint; receiving it does not re-enable consent. The relay application does not keep request or token logs. Network infrastructure still processes connection IP addresses to deliver and protect HTTPS requests; these addresses are not included in hint payloads or used for analytics.

Analytics you choose to share

Homespun asks once, during setup, whether you want to share anonymous analytics with its developer, and you can change your answer any time in the app under Server › Share Analytics. Nothing is sent unless you choose Share Analytics. If you do, the app sends:

  • Usage counts, with numbers rounded: which setup steps you went through, whether pairing worked, your server's kind and version, how many items a backup or Free Up Space handled and roughly how large they were, which features were used, and short error codes.
  • Crash and performance reports that iOS gives the app (MetricKit), such as where it crashed or hung and how long it took to start. iOS provides these only if Share With App Developers is on in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.
  • The app's version, the iPhone model and iOS version, and a random identifier that's replaced whenever you stop sharing, so data from before and after can't be connected.

Analytics never include photos, videos, file names, places, album names, your server's name or address, or anything else that identifies you. They're used only to find problems and improve Homespun, never for advertising, and never combined with data from other companies.

Problem reports

When you use Report a Problem (Server › Help and About), the app sends what you write, the email address you give (optional, used only to reply), and, unless you turn it off, diagnostics you can read before sending: the app and iOS versions, item counts, your settings, your server's kind, version and check results, a summary of recent backups, the app's log from that session, and iOS's latest crash report. Like analytics, they never include photos, file names, or your server's name or address. Reports are sent whether or not you share analytics, because you send them on purpose.

A report without an email includes your analytics install identifier only while you share analytics. A report with an email never includes that identifier, so it cannot link your analytics to your reply address. The email, message, and any included crash or diagnostic data can be linked to you; analytics, performance data and device identifiers are not linked to your identity.

The demo

Try the Demo uses sample photos that Homespun draws on your iPhone (and a few videos that come with the app) and a pretend server inside the app. The drawn photos are deleted the next time Homespun starts. In the demo, Homespun doesn't read or change your Photos library, records no analytics, sends no problem reports, and pairs with no real server. Leaving deletes the demo's settings and records; the drawn samples stay until the next launch. Your own server's settings and records stay unchanged.

Local demo-time intervals are kept only to exclude delayed or mixed iOS diagnostics from sharing. These intervals stay on the iPhone. The demo never enrolls for remote notifications.

Where it goes and how long it's kept

Analytics, crash reports, and problem reports go to a service the developer runs on Cloudflare (telemetry.homespun.photos), and problem reports are also emailed to the developer. Cloudflare processes this data on the developer's behalf, under obligations that provide the same protection described here. Usage counts are kept for about 13 months, performance reports for 3 months, crash reports for 12 months, and problem reports for 2 years, then deleted. To delete shared analytics and reports associated with an install identifier sooner, open Server › Share Analytics in the app and choose Delete Shared Data. Reports with an email have no install identifier: to delete one, email support@homespun.photos with its report number. You can also use that address to request deletion of any report.

The updated erasure service retains a permanent anti-replay marker containing only a domain-separated SHA-256 digest of the random analytics install identifier, without the raw identifier, a timestamp or links to other identifiers. This marker is used only to stop late uploads recreating erased data. Separately, cleanup records for an unsettled upload may retain its object reference, associated random install identifier, size and times beyond normal retention, potentially indefinitely until settlement and cleanup can be proven. Erasure acknowledges fencing and deletion of observed content, not instantaneous physical deletion of writes still in flight; their finishing writer or scheduled reconciliation removes late objects. No report or diagnostic content is retained in the anti-replay marker.

This website

This website uses no cookies, analytics, or third-party scripts. It is hosted by Cloudflare, which processes technical data such as IP addresses to deliver and protect the site, as described in Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Purchases

Version 1.0.0 has no in-app purchases and sends no purchase events. Version 1.1.0 enables a one-time Premium purchase with Family Sharing for Automatic Mode, widgets, Apple Watch and OneDrive Cloud Copies. Apple processes payment; the developer never receives card or payment-account details. The app checks entitlement on the device. Only if you share analytics can anonymous Premium feature or purchase-result events be included; no transaction identifier or Apple Account is sent in those events. Manual backup, quality settings, verification and Free Up Space remain free.
